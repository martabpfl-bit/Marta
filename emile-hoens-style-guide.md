# Style Guide: Emile Hoens (@emilehoens)

Interior & architectural photographer, Amsterdam. Exclusive real estate, hotels, contemporary photo art.
Source: visual analysis of 5 Instagram screenshots (web research found no interviews or gear info, so nothing here is sourced from him directly).

## 1. The look in one sentence
Warm, low-key, cinematic interiors: honeyed natural light, deep chocolate/espresso shadows, muted earthy palette, calm and quiet like a film still, but sharp and architectural.

## 2. Light
- Prefers natural light, often golden hour / low sun streaming through large windows (Minotti/LC4 post).
- Long soft shadow bars and light stripes on walls and floor create rhythm.
- Low-key exposure: highlights protected (sky and window views kept with detail), shadows allowed to go deep and rich, never crushed to pure black.
- Interiors lit by small practicals (wall sconces, pendants) that glow warmly; ambient fill is gentle.
- Dim, moody scenes (brown niche, old school benches) use directional light falling off to dark.

## 3. Color grade
- Dominant palette: caramel, walnut, cocoa, taupe, sand, cream. Greens appear only as deep, desaturated olive/forest.
- Warm white balance (about 5500-6500K feel), orange/amber highlights, slight warm-magenta in shadows.
- Teal/blue only in small amounts (sky through windows, pool water) as a counterpoint.
- Saturation: low overall, but oranges/reds/wood tones are rich. Accent pops are tiny (red tulips against brown wall).
- Whites are creamy, never clinical. Blacks are lifted slightly and tinted brown.

Starting grade (Lightroom-style, adjust per image):
- Temp +8 to +15 warmer than neutral, Tint +3 to +6 magenta
- Exposure -0.2 to -0.5, Highlights -40 to -70, Shadows +10 to +30, Whites -10, Blacks -10 to -20
- Contrast +10 to +20 (soft, filmic curve with lifted black point)
- Vibrance +5, Saturation -10 to -15 overall
- HSL: Orange sat +10 / lum -5; Yellow hue toward orange, sat -10; Blue sat -20 / lum -15; Green sat -35, hue toward yellow
- Color grading: shadows warm brown (hue ~30, sat 15-20), highlights golden (hue ~45, sat 10-15)
- Subtle vignette -10 to -15; fine grain 8-12 (very light)

## 4. Composition
- Strong geometry: verticals kept straight (architectural correction), horizon level.
- Wide-to-normal perspective (roughly 16-35mm equivalent) for rooms; tighter detail frames for vignettes.
- Layered depth: foreground object (lounge chair, chair back), midground (table), background (window view/city).
- Window as frame: the view outside is part of the composition; large glazing grids give graphic structure.
- Symmetry and centered compositions for niches/shelves; negative space of plain wall.
- Styled but lived-in: open book, vase with flowers, candles, objects on shelves, a person occasionally for scale and mood (back turned or looking out, anonymous).
- Uses vertical 4:5 crops for Instagram.

## 5. Subject & styling
- Design icons (Le Corbusier LC4, Minotti, sculptural wood furniture, round tables, curved oak vanity).
- Natural materials: oak, walnut, marble, plaster/limewash walls, linen sheers, rattan.
- Heritage architecture next to modern interiors (Amsterdam brick, Jugendstil doors, arched windows).
- Hotels and wellness: pools with reflections, bars with brass pendants and arched wood.

## 6. Mood
Quiet, luxurious, nostalgic, editorial. Slight cinematic drama in the dark/low-key shots. Never HDR-flat or bright-and-airy.

## 7. Do / Don't
Do: warm light, protected highlights, rich shadows, earthy palette, straight lines, one small color accent, window views.
Don't: cool/blue white balance, flat bright "real-estate" lighting, oversaturated greens, HDR halos, wide-angle distortion, clutter.

## 8. Prompt block (for AI image generation / briefing other photographers)
> Architectural interior photograph, warm golden-hour sunlight streaming through floor-to-ceiling windows, long soft shadows on wall and wooden floor, low-key cinematic exposure, deep chocolate and caramel tones, creamy whites, muted desaturated palette with rich wood, designer furniture (Minotti / Le Corbusier lounge chair), straight vertical lines, 24mm perspective-corrected, layered depth, subtle film grain, quiet luxurious editorial mood, shot on a full-frame camera, 4:5.

Negative: oversaturated, HDR, cold blue tones, harsh flash, fisheye distortion, clutter, people facing the camera.
